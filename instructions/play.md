Lockpicking replaces the d20 check with thieves' tools when someone picks a lock; a trap stays a d20 check. Give a lock its lock-dc when it enters the story, from what the lock is, never from who will try it. Call lockpicking-pick only when the lock stands between the characters and something they want, and put the stake in its question, the patrol on the stairs or the noise: a failure costs time. When lockpicking says thieves' tools are needed, no one without them in their inventory can try: say so, and roll nothing; below that anyone may, with a wire or a hairpin, and proficiency only gives the rerolls. Tell the dice as pins giving way one by one, a 1 as the spring snapping back, a reroll as the hand catching it in time, the wear as a mechanism that grinds; never the DC. What breaks a lock sooner, or keeps it from ever jamming, is yours to rule: write it on its page.
