Lockpicking replaces the d20 check with thieves' tools when someone picks a lock; a trap stays a d20 check. Give a lock its lock-dc when it enters the story, from what the lock is, never from who will try it. Call lockpicking-pick only when the lock stands between the characters and something they want, and put the stake in its question, the patrol on the stairs or the noise: a failure costs time. Who is proficient with thieves' tools needs them from DC 15: check they carry them. Anyone else may always try, at any DC, with a tool from their inventory, given as tool: what counts is yours to rule, thieves' tools, a knife or a screwdriver, never bare fingers; with none, the lock is forced or broken. Tell the dice as pins giving way one by one, a 1 as the spring snapping back, a reroll as the hand catching it in time, the wear as a mechanism that grinds; never the DC. What breaks a lock sooner, or keeps it from ever jamming, is yours to rule: write it on its page.
